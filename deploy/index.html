Getting Licensed in Washington

Washington State has one of the most straightforward PI licensing processes in the country.

Requirement Washington
Minimum Age 18 (21 for armed)
Required Experience None for unarmed PI
Training 4-hour pre-assignment course
Exam Short test after training (not a state board exam)
Background Check Fingerprint-based FBI/state check
Employment Must have job offer from a licensed agency
Cost $200 application fee
Timeline to Licensed Weeks, not months or years

Source: Washington State Department of Licensing — Private Investigators | RCW 18.165

To open your own agency later, you'll need to meet additional requirements. But to start working and earning? A 4-hour course, $200, and a job offer.

What Each Job Actually Looks Like

No vague descriptions. Here's exactly what you'd do, step by step, for each type of work.

Background Checks — The Bread & Butter

What You Get

A name, DOB, maybe a SSN last-4, and what the client needs to know — employment history? Criminal? Financial? All of the above?

What You Actually Do

  • Log into investigative databases (Tracers, TLOxp, IRBSearch — $100-300/month subscription)
  • Pull records: criminal history (federal + state + county), civil suits, liens, judgments, bankruptcies, sex offender registries, professional licenses
  • Cross-reference: verify employment history, education credentials, address history
  • Check social media — public posts, photos, associations, lifestyle vs. claims
  • Sometimes call former employers or references to verify claims
  • Write a clean report summarizing findings
Time per case:

2-4 hours for a standard background check

What you earn:

$300-800 per check depending on depth. Through an agency: $150-400 per case or $50-75/hr

This is the puzzle-solving Heidi loved in the eviction case — piecing together who someone really is from scattered data points.

Skip Tracing — Finding People Who Don't Want to Be Found

What You Get

A name, last known address, maybe some old contact info. The person owes money, skipped bail, is a missing heir, or is a witness needed for a case.

What You Actually Do

  • Database searches — address history, phone records, utility connections, property records
  • Social media deep-dive — who are they connected to? Whose photos are they tagged in? What locations are geotagged?
  • Public records — voter registration, vehicle registration, professional licenses (people forget to update these)
  • Associate analysis — find their family and friends, check their records for connections back to the subject
  • Sometimes a pretext call (legal) to verify they're at the located address
  • Deliver the located address/phone with a confidence rating and supporting evidence
Time per case:

1-6 hours depending on difficulty

What you earn:

$300-800 basic. $2,500-10,000 for complex missing person cases. Agency cut: $150-500 per locate or hourly

This is literally what Heidi did with the eviction tenant. Found the real identity, connected the dots, traced the person. She already knows how to do this.

Insurance Fraud / SIU — Where Heidi's Medical Background Is Gold

Why This Is Heidi's Money Niche

Insurance companies desperately need investigators who can read medical records and spot what doesn't add up. Most PIs can't do this. Heidi can. Insurance fraud costs the industry $80+ billion a year — they pay real money for someone who can read the charts and explain why a claim is inconsistent.

What You Get

A claim file — claimant says they're injured, can't work, needs surgery. Something flagged it as suspicious and the insurance company needs someone who understands medicine to review it.

What You Actually Do

  • Review medical records — do the treatment notes match the claimed injury? Are the billing codes appropriate? Is the provider ordering unnecessary procedures?
  • Check provider history — has this doctor been flagged before? Pattern of suspicious claims from the same clinic?
  • Cross-reference activity — claimant says they can't lift 10 lbs but their social media shows them at CrossFit
  • Write a medical opinion summary explaining what's clinically inconsistent
  • Sometimes paired with a surveillance investigator who gets the video while you do the medical analysis
Time per case:

4-10 hours for medical record review

What Heidi could earn:

Through an agency as clinical fraud investigator: $65,000-$125,000/year
As 1099 contractor with medical expertise: $75-150/hr
Combined with Legal Nurse Consulting: $125-200/hr

Insurance companies post job listings that literally say "RN required" or "nursing background preferred." Heidi has 15+ years clinical experience AND a Masters. She's not competing with random PIs — she's in a specialized lane where the supply of qualified people is tiny and the demand is constant.

How Work Finds You

You don't have to become marketers. You don't need a website, business cards, or a social media presence to start. Here's how PIs actually get work:

Option A: Affiliate with an Existing Agency (Recommended to Start)

This works exactly like real estate. A real estate agent hangs their license with a brokerage — the brokerage has the clients, the reputation, and the deal flow. The agent picks which listings to take, works their own schedule, and splits the commission. PI agencies work the same way.

You affiliate as a 1099 independent contractor — not a W-2 employee. The agency feeds you cases. You choose which ones to accept. You decline anything that doesn't fit your schedule. They handle client billing and intake, you do the investigation work, they take a cut (typically 30-40%). No set hours, no shifts, no boss breathing down your neck. This is not another job — it's a case pipeline you tap into when you're available.

Agencies actively hiring in the Seattle/Tacoma area right now:

  • ICS (International Counterintelligence Services) — national firm, employs subcontractors in WA. icsworld.com
  • DigiStream Investigations — hiring investigators in Seattle/Tacoma/Everett region. Indeed.com
  • CoventBridge Group — Seattle area, pays for your licensing fees. Glassdoor
  • Lemieux & Associates — hiring SIU field investigators in Tacoma, part-time. Glassdoor

Option B: Process Serving Companies (Immediate Income)

Process serving companies always need people. Sign up as an independent contractor, pick up serves in your area. No experience needed, work when you want.

  • ABC Legal — Seattle-based, largest process serving company in the US. Sign up as independent contractor, 1099 work, set your own schedule. abclegal.com/serve
  • ServeNow — National network. Register as a server in WA, get overflow work. serve-now.com

WA requirement: Register with the auditor of your county to serve process.

Option C: Investigation Networks (Cases Come to You)

Once licensed, list yourself on investigation referral networks. Attorneys and companies search these when they need a PI in your area.

  • PInow.com — the largest PI referral network. Attorneys, insurance companies, and corporations search by location. You get phone and email leads. pinow.com
  • NALI — National Association of Legal Investigators. Professional network, attorney referrals.
  • Insurance SIU Panels — Insurance companies maintain approved vendor lists of PIs. Get on the list, cases come automatically.

The bottom line: Affiliate with an agency as a 1099 contractor. No marketing. No website. No cold calls. No set schedule. No boss. They have the clients — you pick the cases that fit your week. Your reputation builds on results, not on self-promotion.
